Peace Lily Care Guide

Spathiphyllum wallisii

EasyToxic to pets

Peace Lily (Spathiphyllum wallisii) asks for very little. The trouble is that very little gets read as nothing, and then it gets watered on a schedule it never wanted. The first thing that usually goes wrong is dramatic full wilt — underwatered. It wants low to bright indirect light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Low to bright indirect. Blooms more in brighter light; survives in dim corners.
Water
Keep lightly moist. It wilts dramatically when thirsty and recovers within hours of watering.
Humidity
50%+. Appreciates a humid bathroom.
Soil
Standard potting mix that holds some moisture.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute feed every 6-8 weeks in spring and summer.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Insoluble calcium oxalates. Oral irritation, drooling, difficulty swallowing. Not a true lily and not kidney-toxic to cats, unlike Lilium.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Peace Lily dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: keep lightly moist. It wilts dramatically when thirsty and recovers within hours of watering.

When something looks wrong

Dramatic full wilt

Usually: Underwatered

Water thoroughly. It usually recovers completely within a few hours.

Brown leaf tips

Usually: Tap water minerals, fluoride, or dry air

Try filtered or left-out water; raise humidity.

No flowers

Usually: Too little light

Move to brighter indirect light.

Worth knowing

Its wilting is a useful signal, not a crisis — this is the one plant that reliably tells you when it is thirsty.
