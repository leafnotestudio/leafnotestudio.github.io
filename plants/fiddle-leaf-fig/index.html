Fiddle Leaf Fig Care Guide

Ficus lyrata

TrickyToxic to pets

Fiddle Leaf Fig (Ficus lyrata) is demanding, and worth it to the people who find it worth it. It will not accept a watering schedule. The first thing that usually goes wrong is brown spots spreading from leaf edges — usually overwatering or root rot. It wants bright, some direct light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright light, including some direct morning sun. Needs more light than most people give it.
Water
When the top 2 inches are dry. Consistency matters more than volume — it hates swings.
Humidity
40-60%.
Soil
Well-draining potting mix.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Balanced feed monthly in spring and summer.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Ficus sap irritates skin, mouth and gut. Causes vomiting and drooling if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Fiddle Leaf Fig dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: when the top 2 inches are dry. Consistency matters more than volume — it hates swings.

When something looks wrong

Brown spots spreading from leaf edges

Usually: Usually overwatering or root rot

Check roots. Let it dry further between waterings.

Dropping leaves suddenly

Usually: Shock from being moved, a draft, or a temperature swing

Pick one bright spot and leave it there. It dislikes being relocated.

Worth knowing

The hardest plant here. It wants one bright position, a consistent watering rhythm, and no drafts. Pick its spot carefully and then stop fussing.
