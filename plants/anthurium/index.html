Anthurium Care Guide

Anthurium andraeanum

ModerateToxic to pets

Anthurium (Anthurium andraeanum) rewards a bit of attention. It is not fussy enough to be frustrating, but it will not thrive on autopilot either. The first thing that usually goes wrong is green rather than red blooms — normal ageing of the spathe, or too little light. It wants bright indirect light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright indirect. No direct sun.
Water
Keep lightly moist; let the top inch dry between waterings.
Humidity
60%+. More humidity means more blooms.
Soil
Very chunky orchid-style mix with bark — it is semi-epiphytic.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
High-phosphorus feed diluted, every 6-8 weeks in the growing season.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Insoluble calcium oxalates. Oral irritation, drooling and swelling if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Anthurium dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: keep lightly moist; let the top inch dry between waterings.

When something looks wrong

Green rather than red blooms

Usually: Normal ageing of the spathe, or too little light

Nothing if the bloom is old. Otherwise increase light.

Yellowing leaves

Usually: Overwatering in too dense a substrate

Repot into a chunkier, barkier mix.

Worth knowing

The red 'flower' is a modified leaf and lasts weeks. Plant it in bark, not dense soil — most Anthurium deaths are a potting mix problem.
