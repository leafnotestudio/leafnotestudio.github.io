Alocasia Zebrina Care Guide

Alocasia zebrina

TrickyToxic to pets

Alocasia Zebrina (Alocasia zebrina) is the kind of plant that looks fine for three weeks and then all at once does not. Problems start well before they show. The first thing that usually goes wrong is fine webbing and stippled leaves — spider mites, which target Alocasia relentlessly. It wants bright indirect light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright indirect. No direct sun on the leaves.
Water
Lightly moist in summer, much drier in winter. Hates sitting wet.
Humidity
60%+.
Soil
Very airy, fast-draining mix with bark and perlite.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute feed monthly in spring and summer only.
Toxic to cats and dogs

High insoluble calcium oxalate content. Significant oral irritation, drooling and swelling if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Alocasia Zebrina dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: lightly moist in summer, much drier in winter. Hates sitting wet.

When something looks wrong

Fine webbing and stippled leaves

Usually: Spider mites, which target Alocasia relentlessly

Rinse both leaf surfaces, treat with insecticidal soap weekly for three weeks, raise humidity.

Losing every leaf in autumn

Usually: Usually dormancy, not death

Cut water right back, keep the corm warm and barely damp, wait for spring.

Worth knowing

The striped stems are the point - give it room so they are visible rather than crowded against other plants.
