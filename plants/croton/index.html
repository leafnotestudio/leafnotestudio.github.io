Croton Care Guide

Codiaeum variegatum

ModerateToxic to pets

Croton (Codiaeum variegatum) sits between easy and demanding: it notices when something is wrong, and it shows you reasonably quickly. The first thing that usually goes wrong is dropping all its leaves after you bring it home — shock from the change in light and humidity. It wants bright, some direct light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright light with some direct sun. Colour is entirely light-dependent.
Water
Keep lightly moist. Let the top inch dry, no more.
Humidity
50%+.
Soil
Well-draining potting mix.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Balanced feed monthly in spring and summer.
Toxic to cats and dogs

The milky sap irritates skin, mouth and gut. Causes vomiting and drooling if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Croton dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: keep lightly moist. Let the top inch dry, no more.

When something looks wrong

Dropping all its leaves after you bring it home

Usually: Shock from the change in light and humidity

Pick a bright spot and leave it alone. New growth usually follows within a few weeks.

New leaves coming in plain green

Usually: Not enough light

Move brighter. The reds and yellows only develop with strong light.

Worth knowing

Expect it to drop leaves when you first bring it home. That is shock, not death, and it recovers if you stop moving it around.
