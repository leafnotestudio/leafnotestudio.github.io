Bird of Paradise Care Guide

Strelitzia nicolai

ModerateToxic to pets

Bird of Paradise (Strelitzia nicolai) sits between easy and demanding: it notices when something is wrong, and it shows you reasonably quickly. The first thing that usually goes wrong is leaves splitting along the sides — completely normal - it is how the plant survives wind. It wants bright, direct sun light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
The brightest spot you have, including several hours of direct sun. It is a full-sun plant outdoors.
Water
Water thoroughly when the top 2 inches dry. Large leaves move a lot of water in summer.
Humidity
40-60%.
Soil
Rich, well-draining potting mix.
Temperature
65-85 °F
Feeding
Balanced feed monthly spring through summer. It is a heavy feeder for a houseplant.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Toxic to cats and dogs. The seeds and fruit are the worst part. Causes vomiting and drowsiness.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Bird of Paradise dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: water thoroughly when the top 2 inches dry. Large leaves move a lot of water in summer.

When something looks wrong

Leaves splitting along the sides

Usually: Completely normal - it is how the plant survives wind

Nothing. Splits are not damage and cannot be prevented.

No new growth, pale leaves

Usually: Not enough light. This is the usual indoor failure.

Move to the brightest window in the house. It cannot thrive in medium light.

Worth knowing

Indoor plants rarely flower - they typically need several years, a lot of direct sun, and to be root-bound. Treat it as a foliage plant.
