Swiss Cheese Vine Care Guide

Monstera adansonii

EasyToxic to pets

Swiss Cheese Vine (Monstera adansonii) is hard to kill, which is how most of them die: it tolerates neglect well enough that people stop looking at it. The first thing that usually goes wrong is long gaps between leaves — insufficient light. It wants bright indirect light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright indirect. No direct sun.
Water
Water when the top inch is dry. Dries faster than M. deliciosa because of thinner leaves.
Humidity
50-70%. Noticeably happier above 50%.
Soil
Airy mix with bark and perlite.
Temperature
65-85 °F
Feeding
Balanced liquid feed monthly, spring through summer.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Insoluble calcium oxalates. Oral irritation and vomiting if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Swiss Cheese Vine dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: water when the top inch is dry. Dries faster than M. deliciosa because of thinner leaves.

When something looks wrong

Long gaps between leaves

Usually: Insufficient light

Move brighter. Leggy growth will not fill back in — prune and propagate instead.

Crispy edges on new leaves

Usually: Low humidity

Raise ambient humidity; avoid placing near heating vents.

Worth knowing

Give it something to climb. Vining up a pole produces larger leaves with more holes than trailing down.
