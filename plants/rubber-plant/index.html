Rubber Plant Care Guide

Ficus elastica

EasyToxic to pets

If you have killed houseplants before, Rubber Plant (Ficus elastica) is a reasonable place to start again. It recovers from most mistakes. The first thing that usually goes wrong is lower leaves yellow and drop — overwatering or a cold draft. It wants bright indirect light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright indirect. Variegated forms need more light than plain green.
Water
When the top 2 inches dry out. Less in winter.
Humidity
40-60%.
Soil
Well-draining potting mix with perlite.
Temperature
60-80 °F
Feeding
Balanced feed monthly in the growing season.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Milky latex sap. Irritates mouth and gut; causes vomiting if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Rubber Plant dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: when the top 2 inches dry out. Less in winter.

When something looks wrong

Lower leaves yellow and drop

Usually: Overwatering or a cold draft

Let the soil dry further; move away from drafty windows and doors.

Dusty, dull leaves

Usually: Dust buildup blocks light

Wipe leaves with a damp cloth monthly. Makes a visible difference.

Worth knowing

Far more forgiving than its cousin the fiddle leaf fig, and nearly as striking.
