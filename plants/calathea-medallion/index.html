Calathea Medallion Care Guide

Goeppertia veitchiana

TrickyPet safe

Calathea Medallion (Goeppertia veitchiana) is the kind of plant that looks fine for three weeks and then all at once does not. Problems start well before they show. The first thing that usually goes wrong is crispy brown edges — dry air or minerals in tap water. It wants medium indirect light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Medium indirect. Direct sun bleaches the pattern.
Water
Keep evenly moist with filtered or dechlorinated water. Never soggy, never bone dry.
Humidity
60%+. Non-negotiable for clean leaf edges.
Soil
Moisture-retentive but airy mix.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute feed monthly in spring and summer.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Calathea Medallion dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: keep evenly moist with filtered or dechlorinated water. Never soggy, never bone dry.

When something looks wrong

Crispy brown edges

Usually: Dry air or minerals in tap water

Raise humidity above 60%. Switch to filtered or distilled water.

Leaves curling inward

Usually: Thirsty, or the air is too dry

Water and raise humidity together - usually both are involved.

Worth knowing

The undersides are deep purple and the leaves lift at night, so it is worth a spot where you see it from more than one angle.
