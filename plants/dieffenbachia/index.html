Dieffenbachia Care Guide

Dieffenbachia seguine

EasyToxic to pets

Dieffenbachia (Dieffenbachia seguine) asks for very little. The trouble is that very little gets read as nothing, and then it gets watered on a schedule it never wanted. The first thing that usually goes wrong is bare leggy stem with leaves only at the top — normal with age, accelerated by low light. It wants medium to bright indirect light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Medium to bright indirect. Tolerates lower light with slower growth.
Water
When the top inch is dry.
Humidity
40-60%.
Soil
Standard potting mix with perlite.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Balanced feed monthly in the growing season.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Among the more severe. High calcium oxalate content causes intense oral pain, drooling and swelling that can affect swallowing. Keep well away from pets and small children.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Dieffenbachia dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: when the top inch is dry.

When something looks wrong

Bare leggy stem with leaves only at the top

Usually: Normal with age, accelerated by low light

Cut the cane back hard. It resprouts from the stump, and the cut top roots in water.

Yellowing lower leaves

Usually: Overwatering or natural leaf drop

Let the soil dry further between waterings.

Worth knowing

This is the one on the list worth being genuinely careful with. The sap causes painful mouth swelling, which is where the old name dumb cane comes from. Wash your hands after pruning.
