Golden Pothos Care Guide

Epipremnum aureum

EasyToxic to pets

Golden Pothos (Epipremnum aureum) is forgiving about almost everything except one thing, and knowing which thing is most of the job. The first thing that usually goes wrong is variegation turning solid green — too little light. It wants low to bright indirect light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Adapts from low to bright indirect. Variegation fades in low light.
Water
Let the top half of the pot dry out. Tolerates neglect far better than overwatering.
Humidity
Any. Unfussy.
Soil
Standard potting mix with added perlite.
Temperature
60-85 °F
Feeding
Dilute balanced feed every 6-8 weeks in the growing season.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Insoluble calcium oxalates. Oral irritation, drooling, vomiting if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Golden Pothos dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: let the top half of the pot dry out. Tolerates neglect far better than overwatering.

When something looks wrong

Variegation turning solid green

Usually: Too little light

Move to brighter indirect light. Prune back to a variegated node.

Black stems, mushy base

Usually: Root rot from overwatering

Unpot, cut away rotted roots, repot in fresh dry mix. Propagate healthy cuttings as insurance.

Worth knowing

The most forgiving plant on this list. If you kill a pothos, the cause is almost always too much water, not too little.
