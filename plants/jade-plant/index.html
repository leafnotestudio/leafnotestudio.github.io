Jade Plant Care Guide

Crassula ovata

EasyToxic to pets

Jade Plant (Crassula ovata) is hard to kill, which is how most of them die: it tolerates neglect well enough that people stop looking at it. The first thing that usually goes wrong is dropping leaves at a touch — usually overwatering; sometimes a sudden move. It wants bright, direct sun light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright light with several hours of direct sun.
Water
Let the soil dry fully between waterings. Every 2-3 weeks in summer, monthly or less in winter.
Humidity
Any. Prefers dry.
Soil
Cactus or succulent mix.
Temperature
60-80 °F
Feeding
Succulent feed twice in the growing season.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Toxic to cats and dogs. Causes vomiting, incoordination and lethargy. The toxic principle is not fully identified.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Jade Plant dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: let the soil dry fully between waterings. Every 2-3 weeks in summer, monthly or less in winter.

When something looks wrong

Dropping leaves at a touch

Usually: Usually overwatering; sometimes a sudden move

Let it dry out thoroughly and reduce frequency.

Stretched, leaning stems

Usually: Not enough light

Move to direct sun. Prune leggy growth; cuttings root easily.

Worth knowing

Can live for decades and be pruned like a bonsai. Treat it as a sun plant, not a shelf plant.
