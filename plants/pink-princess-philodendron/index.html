Pink Princess Philodendron Care Guide

Philodendron erubescens Pink Princess

ModerateToxic to pets

Pink Princess Philodendron (Philodendron erubescens Pink Princess) rewards a bit of attention. It is not fussy enough to be frustrating, but it will not thrive on autopilot either. The first thing that usually goes wrong is all-green leaves with no pink — too little light, or the plant is reverting. It wants bright indirect light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright indirect, and plenty of it. Light drives the pink.
Water
When the top inch is dry. Do not let it sit wet.
Humidity
55%+.
Soil
Chunky aroid mix with bark, perlite and a little coir.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Balanced feed monthly in the growing season.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Insoluble calcium oxalates. Oral irritation, drooling and vomiting if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Pink Princess Philodendron dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: when the top inch is dry. Do not let it sit wet.

When something looks wrong

All-green leaves with no pink

Usually: Too little light, or the plant is reverting

More bright indirect light. Prune back to a node showing variegation.

An entirely pink leaf that browns and dies

Usually: No chlorophyll, so the leaf cannot feed itself

Expected. Do not chase all-pink leaves - a half-and-half leaf is the healthy ideal.

Worth knowing

Aim for roughly half pink per leaf. Fully pink leaves look spectacular in photos and then die, because they have no chlorophyll.
