Spider Plant Care Guide

Chlorophytum comosum

EasyPet safe

Spider Plant (Chlorophytum comosum) is forgiving about almost everything except one thing, and knowing which thing is most of the job. The first thing that usually goes wrong is brown leaf tips — fluoride or chlorine in tap water. It wants bright indirect light. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Bright indirect. Tolerates medium light.
Water
When the top inch dries. Sensitive to fluoride in tap water.
Humidity
Any.
Soil
Standard potting mix.
Temperature
60-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute feed monthly in spring and summer.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not toxic to cats or dogs. Cats are often drawn to chewing it; this causes no poisoning, though large amounts may upset the stomach.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Spider Plant dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: when the top inch dries. Sensitive to fluoride in tap water.

When something looks wrong

Brown leaf tips

Usually: Fluoride or chlorine in tap water

Use filtered water or leave tap water out overnight.

No babies forming

Usually: Too little light, or pot too large

Brighter light; spider plants pup more readily when slightly root-bound.

Worth knowing

Pet-safe and prolific. The plantlets root in water in about two weeks, which makes it the best plant to give away.
