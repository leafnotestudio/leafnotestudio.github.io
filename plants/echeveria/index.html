Echeveria Care Guide

Echeveria elegans

EasyPet safe

Echeveria (Echeveria elegans) is hard to kill, which is how most of them die: it tolerates neglect well enough that people stop looking at it. The first thing that usually goes wrong is stretching tall with gaps between leaves — not enough light - the most common indoor problem. It wants full direct sun light. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
As much direct sun as you can give it. At least 6 hours.
Water
Soak thoroughly, then let the soil dry out completely. Water the soil, not the rosette.
Humidity
Any. Prefers dry.
Soil
Gritty cactus or succulent mix. Drainage is everything.
Temperature
60-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute succulent feed twice in summer at most.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Echeveria dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: soak thoroughly, then let the soil dry out completely. Water the soil, not the rosette.

When something looks wrong

Stretching tall with gaps between leaves

Usually: Not enough light - the most common indoor problem

Move to the sunniest spot. Behead the stretched rosette and re-root the top.

Mushy translucent lower leaves

Usually: Overwatering, or water sitting in the rosette

Stop watering until bone dry. Always water the soil, never the centre.

Worth knowing

Pet-safe and nearly indestructible given enough sun. The powdery coating on the leaves is a natural sunscreen, so handle by the pot rather than the leaves.
