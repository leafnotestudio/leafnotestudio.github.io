Air Plant Care Guide

Tillandsia ionantha

ModeratePet safe

Air Plant (Tillandsia ionantha) sits between easy and demanding: it notices when something is wrong, and it shows you reasonably quickly. The first thing that usually goes wrong is rotting, falling apart at the base — water trapped in the centre after soaking. It wants bright indirect light. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Bright indirect. No soil, no pot.
Water
Soak in room-temperature water for 20-30 minutes every 1-2 weeks, then shake out and dry upside down within 4 hours.
Humidity
50%+. Mist between soaks in dry homes.
Soil
None. Mount on wood or display in a bowl with airflow.
Temperature
55-85 °F
Feeding
Dilute bromeliad feed in the soak water once a month.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Air Plant dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: soak in room-temperature water for 20-30 minutes every 1-2 weeks, then shake out and dry upside down within 4 hours.

When something looks wrong

Rotting, falling apart at the base

Usually: Water trapped in the centre after soaking

Always dry upside down with good airflow. Never leave it sitting wet in a closed terrarium.

Brown crispy leaf tips

Usually: Not enough water

Soak more often; mist between soaks.

Worth knowing

The drying step matters more than the watering step. Rot from trapped water kills more air plants than drought does.
