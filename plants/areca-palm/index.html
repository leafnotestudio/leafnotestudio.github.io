Areca Palm Care Guide

Dypsis lutescens

ModeratePet safe

Areca Palm (Dypsis lutescens) sits between easy and demanding: it notices when something is wrong, and it shows you reasonably quickly. The first thing that usually goes wrong is brown tips across many fronds — dry air, or fluoride and salts in tap water. It wants bright indirect light. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Bright indirect, with a little direct morning sun tolerated.
Water
Keep lightly moist in summer, drier in winter. Sensitive to fluoride.
Humidity
50%+.
Soil
Well-draining potting mix with perlite.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Balanced feed monthly in spring and summer.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Areca Palm dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: keep lightly moist in summer, drier in winter. Sensitive to fluoride.

When something looks wrong

Brown tips across many fronds

Usually: Dry air, or fluoride and salts in tap water

Raise humidity and switch to filtered water.

Fine webbing between fronds

Usually: Spider mites, which love dry indoor air

Rinse thoroughly, treat with insecticidal soap, raise humidity.

Worth knowing

One of the largest genuinely pet-safe plants you can keep indoors, which is why it turns up in so many homes with cats.
