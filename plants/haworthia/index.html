Haworthia Care Guide

Haworthiopsis attenuata

EasyPet safe

Haworthia (Haworthiopsis attenuata) is forgiving about almost everything except one thing, and knowing which thing is most of the job. The first thing that usually goes wrong is leaves going soft and translucent — overwatering. It wants bright indirect light. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Bright indirect to a little direct sun. More tolerant of lower light than most succulents.
Water
Let the soil dry completely, then water sparingly. Every 2-3 weeks in summer, monthly in winter.
Humidity
Any. Prefers dry.
Soil
Gritty cactus or succulent mix.
Temperature
60-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute succulent feed once or twice in summer.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Haworthia dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: let the soil dry completely, then water sparingly. Every 2-3 weeks in summer, monthly in winter.

When something looks wrong

Leaves going soft and translucent

Usually: Overwatering

Stop watering. Remove mushy leaves. Repot into dry gritty mix if the base is soft.

Red or purple tinge to the leaves

Usually: Sun stress - usually harmless

Nothing needed unless leaves are also scorching, in which case reduce direct sun.

Worth knowing

One of the best desk plants there is: pet-safe, happy in a small pot, tolerant of less light than most succulents, and nearly impossible to underwater.
