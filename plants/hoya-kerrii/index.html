Sweetheart Hoya Care Guide

Hoya kerrii

EasyPet safe

Sweetheart Hoya (Hoya kerrii) is hard to kill, which is how most of them die: it tolerates neglect well enough that people stop looking at it. The first thing that usually goes wrong is single leaf never grows anything — a single-leaf cutting sold without a node cannot produce a vine. It wants bright, some direct light. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Bright indirect with some direct sun.
Water
Let the soil dry out almost completely. The thick leaves store water.
Humidity
40%+.
Soil
Chunky, free-draining mix with bark and perlite.
Temperature
60-85 °F
Feeding
Balanced feed monthly in spring and summer.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Sweetheart Hoya dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: let the soil dry out almost completely. The thick leaves store water.

When something looks wrong

Single leaf never grows anything

Usually: A single-leaf cutting sold without a node cannot produce a vine

Nothing will fix it. It can live for years as a leaf but will never become a plant.

Wrinkled, soft leaves

Usually: Underwatered or root-bound

Water thoroughly and check whether roots fill the pot.

Worth knowing

The single heart-shaped leaf sold as a gift is usually just a leaf with no growth node, so it will stay one leaf forever. Buy one with a vine attached if you want an actual plant.
