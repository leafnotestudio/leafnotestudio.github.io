Chinese Money Plant Care Guide

Pilea peperomioides

EasyPet safe

Chinese Money Plant (Pilea peperomioides) asks for very little. The trouble is that very little gets read as nothing, and then it gets watered on a schedule it never wanted. The first thing that usually goes wrong is leaning heavily to one side — phototropism — normal. It wants bright indirect light. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Bright indirect. Rotate weekly or it leans hard toward the light.
Water
When the top inch is dry. Leaves droop slightly when thirsty.
Humidity
Any average household level.
Soil
Well-draining potting mix.
Temperature
60-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute feed monthly in spring and summer.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Chinese Money Plant dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: when the top inch is dry. Leaves droop slightly when thirsty.

When something looks wrong

Leaning heavily to one side

Usually: Phototropism — normal

Rotate a quarter turn weekly.

Pale, cupping leaves

Usually: Too much direct sun

Move back from the glass into indirect light.

Worth knowing

Produces pups at the base that can be separated and potted. One plant becomes many within a year.
