String of Pearls Care Guide

Curio rowleyanus

TrickyToxic to pets

String of Pearls (Curio rowleyanus) is demanding, and worth it to the people who find it worth it. It will not accept a watering schedule. The first thing that usually goes wrong is pearls shrivelling and going flat — underwatered, or roots have died from earlier overwatering. It wants bright, some direct light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright light including a few hours of direct sun.
Water
Let the soil dry completely, then water sparingly. More die from overwatering than anything else.
Humidity
Any. Prefers dry.
Soil
Gritty cactus or succulent mix in a shallow pot.
Temperature
60-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute succulent feed twice in the growing season.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Toxic to cats and dogs. Causes vomiting, drooling and skin irritation on contact.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a String of Pearls dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: let the soil dry completely, then water sparingly. More die from overwatering than anything else.

When something looks wrong

Pearls shrivelling and going flat

Usually: Underwatered, or roots have died from earlier overwatering

Water thoroughly once. If there is no improvement in a week, check the roots.

Mushy pearls and bare strands

Usually: Overwatering

Stop watering. Cut healthy strands and lay them on dry soil to re-root.

Worth knowing

The roots are shallow, so a deep pot holds wet soil under them and rots the plant. Use a shallow pot and water less than feels right.
