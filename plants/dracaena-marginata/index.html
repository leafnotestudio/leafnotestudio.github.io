Dragon Tree Care Guide

Dracaena marginata

EasyToxic to pets

Dragon Tree (Dracaena marginata) is hard to kill, which is how most of them die: it tolerates neglect well enough that people stop looking at it. The first thing that usually goes wrong is brown tips on otherwise healthy leaves — fluoride and salts in tap water. It wants medium to bright indirect light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Medium to bright indirect. Tolerates lower light with slower growth.
Water
Let the top half of the pot dry out. Sensitive to fluoride in tap water.
Humidity
40%+.
Soil
Well-draining potting mix.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute feed every 8 weeks in the growing season.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Saponins. Causes vomiting, drooling and loss of appetite. Cats may also show dilated pupils.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Dragon Tree dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: let the top half of the pot dry out. Sensitive to fluoride in tap water.

When something looks wrong

Brown tips on otherwise healthy leaves

Usually: Fluoride and salts in tap water

Use filtered or distilled water. Trim tips at an angle with scissors.

Yellowing lower leaves dropping off

Usually: Normal ageing at the base of the cane

Nothing. Remove them once fully yellow.

Worth knowing

Very tolerant of neglect but genuinely fussy about tap water. If the tips keep browning despite good watering, the water is the cause.
