Maidenhair Fern Care Guide

Adiantum raddianum

TrickyPet safe

Maidenhair Fern (Adiantum raddianum) is demanding, and worth it to the people who find it worth it. It will not accept a watering schedule. The first thing that usually goes wrong is whole fronds crisping to brown — it dried out, or the air is too dry. It wants medium indirect light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Medium indirect. Direct sun kills it quickly.
Water
Never let it dry out, not even once. Keep the soil evenly damp at all times.
Humidity
60-70%+.
Soil
Moisture-retentive, airy mix.
Temperature
60-75 °F
Feeding
Very dilute feed every 6-8 weeks in the growing season.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Maidenhair Fern dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: never let it dry out, not even once. Keep the soil evenly damp at all times.

When something looks wrong

Whole fronds crisping to brown

Usually: It dried out, or the air is too dry

Cut the dead fronds right back to soil level, water, raise humidity. It regrows from the base.

Looks dead entirely

Usually: Often dormant rather than gone

Trim everything to the soil, keep it damp and warm, and give it a month before giving up.

Worth knowing

The least forgiving plant in the shop. One missed watering can take out every frond, though it usually regrows from the base if you cut back and keep it damp.
