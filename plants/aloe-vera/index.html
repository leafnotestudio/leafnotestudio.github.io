Aloe Vera Care Guide

Aloe barbadensis miller

EasyToxic to pets

Aloe Vera (Aloe barbadensis miller) is hard to kill, which is how most of them die: it tolerates neglect well enough that people stop looking at it. The first thing that usually goes wrong is flat, splaying leaves — too little light. It wants bright, some direct light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright light with some direct sun.
Water
Soak thoroughly, then let the soil dry completely. Every 2-4 weeks.
Humidity
Any. Prefers dry.
Soil
Cactus or succulent mix with grit.
Temperature
60-85 °F
Feeding
Succulent feed once or twice in summer.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Saponins and anthraquinones in the leaf skin and latex. Causes vomiting, diarrhoea and lethargy if eaten by cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Aloe Vera dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: soak thoroughly, then let the soil dry completely. Every 2-4 weeks.

When something looks wrong

Flat, splaying leaves

Usually: Too little light

Move to the brightest spot available.

Soft, translucent, mushy leaves

Usually: Overwatering

Stop watering. Remove affected leaves. Repot in gritty dry mix if the base is soft.

Worth knowing

Toxic to pets despite being a household remedy for humans. Worth stating plainly on the card.
