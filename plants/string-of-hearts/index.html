String of Hearts Care Guide

Ceropegia woodii

EasyPet safe

String of Hearts (Ceropegia woodii) asks for very little. The trouble is that very little gets read as nothing, and then it gets watered on a schedule it never wanted. The first thing that usually goes wrong is long bare stretches of vine — too little light. It wants bright, some direct light. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Bright indirect to some direct sun. More light means tighter spacing and stronger colour.
Water
Let the soil dry fully. Semi-succulent — it stores water in its leaves and tubers.
Humidity
Any. Prefers dry.
Soil
Cactus or succulent mix.
Temperature
60-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute succulent feed twice in the growing season.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a String of Hearts dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: let the soil dry fully. Semi-succulent — it stores water in its leaves and tubers.

When something looks wrong

Long bare stretches of vine

Usually: Too little light

Move brighter. Trim long bare sections and lay cuttings on soil to root.

Mushy leaves near the base

Usually: Overwatering

Let it dry out completely; reduce frequency.

Worth knowing

Hang it high. Vines reach several feet and the heart shape only reads properly when it trails.
