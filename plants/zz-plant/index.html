ZZ Plant Care Guide

Zamioculcas zamiifolia

EasyToxic to pets

ZZ Plant (Zamioculcas zamiifolia) is hard to kill, which is how most of them die: it tolerates neglect well enough that people stop looking at it. The first thing that usually goes wrong is yellowing stalks from the base — overwatering. It wants low to bright indirect light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Low to bright indirect. One of the few genuinely low-light-tolerant plants.
Water
Every 2-4 weeks, only once the soil is dry throughout. Stores water in its rhizomes.
Humidity
Any.
Soil
Well-draining potting mix with perlite or a cactus blend.
Temperature
60-85 °F
Feeding
Dilute feed twice a year. It barely needs it.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Insoluble calcium oxalates. Oral irritation and vomiting if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a ZZ Plant dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: every 2-4 weeks, only once the soil is dry throughout. Stores water in its rhizomes.

When something looks wrong

Yellowing stalks from the base

Usually: Overwatering

Stop watering entirely until the soil is bone dry. Check rhizomes for soft spots.

No new growth for months

Usually: Normal — it is a slow grower

Nothing. Brighter light speeds it up slightly.

Worth knowing

The best plant for a dark office or a forgetful owner. Almost impossible to underwater.
