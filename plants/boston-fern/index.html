Boston Fern Care Guide

Nephrolepis exaltata

ModeratePet safe

Boston Fern (Nephrolepis exaltata) rewards a bit of attention. It is not fussy enough to be frustrating, but it will not thrive on autopilot either. The first thing that usually goes wrong is dropping leaflets all over the floor — air too dry, or it dried out once too often. It wants bright indirect light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Bright indirect. No direct sun.
Water
Keep consistently moist. This is one of the few houseplants that genuinely dislikes drying out.
Humidity
60%+. The single biggest factor in whether it thrives.
Soil
Moisture-retentive peat or coir based mix.
Temperature
60-75 °F
Feeding
Dilute feed monthly in spring and summer.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Boston Fern dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: keep consistently moist. This is one of the few houseplants that genuinely dislikes drying out.

When something looks wrong

Dropping leaflets all over the floor

Usually: Air too dry, or it dried out once too often

Raise humidity, water more consistently, move away from heating vents.

Pale, washed-out fronds

Usually: Too much direct light

Move to bright indirect or filtered light.

Worth knowing

Pet-safe and genuinely lush, but it wants humidity more than any other plant here. A bathroom with a window is close to ideal.
