Bird's Nest Fern Care Guide

Asplenium nidus

EasyPet safe

Bird's Nest Fern (Asplenium nidus) is forgiving about almost everything except one thing, and knowing which thing is most of the job. The first thing that usually goes wrong is brown crispy frond edges — dry air. It wants low to medium indirect light. It is not toxic to cats or dogs.

Light
Low to medium indirect. Direct sun scorches the fronds.
Water
Keep lightly moist. Water the soil, not the central rosette — water sitting in the crown rots it.
Humidity
50%+. Thrives in bathrooms.
Soil
Moisture-retentive, airy mix.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute feed every 6-8 weeks in the growing season.
Safe for cats and dogs

Not known to be toxic to cats or dogs.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Bird's Nest Fern dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: keep lightly moist. Water the soil, not the central rosette — water sitting in the crown rots it.

When something looks wrong

Brown crispy frond edges

Usually: Dry air

Raise humidity; move away from vents and radiators.

Rot at the centre of the rosette

Usually: Water poured into the crown

Always water the soil around the edge of the pot instead.

Worth knowing

The easiest fern to keep alive indoors, and safe around pets. Never water into the middle.
