Monstera Care Guide

Monstera deliciosa

EasyToxic to pets

Monstera (Monstera deliciosa) is forgiving about almost everything except one thing, and knowing which thing is most of the job. The first thing that usually goes wrong is leaves stay whole with no splits — not enough light, or plant is still juvenile. It wants bright indirect light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright indirect. Tolerates a few hours of soft morning sun; harsh afternoon sun scorches leaves.
Water
Water when the top 2 inches of soil are dry. Drench until it runs from the drainage hole, then let it drain fully.
Humidity
40-60%. Tolerates average household humidity.
Soil
Chunky, fast-draining mix — potting soil with bark and perlite.
Temperature
65-85 °F
Feeding
Balanced liquid feed monthly in spring and summer. None in winter.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Insoluble calcium oxalates. Causes oral irritation, drooling and vomiting if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Monstera dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: water when the top 2 inches of soil are dry. Drench until it runs from the drainage hole, then let it drain fully.

When something looks wrong

Leaves stay whole with no splits

Usually: Not enough light, or plant is still juvenile

Move closer to a bright window. Splits develop with maturity.

Yellowing lower leaves

Usually: Overwatering

Let the top layer dry fully before the next drink. Check that drainage is not blocked.

Brown crispy leaf edges

Usually: Dry air or underwatering

Water more consistently; group with other plants to raise local humidity.

Worth knowing

Aerial roots are normal and healthy. Tuck them into the pot or leave them; do not cut them off.
