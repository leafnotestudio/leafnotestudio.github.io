Snake Plant Care Guide

Dracaena trifasciata

EasyToxic to pets

If you have killed houseplants before, Snake Plant (Dracaena trifasciata) is a reasonable place to start again. It recovers from most mistakes. The first thing that usually goes wrong is soft, mushy leaf bases — overwatering — the most common way this plant dies. It wants low to full sun light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Anything from low light to full sun. Grows fastest in bright indirect.
Water
Let the soil dry completely, then water sparingly. Every 2-6 weeks depending on light and season.
Humidity
Any. Prefers dry air.
Soil
Cactus or succulent mix. Drainage is critical.
Temperature
60-85 °F
Feeding
Succulent feed twice during the growing season at most.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Saponins. Causes nausea, vomiting and diarrhoea if eaten.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Snake Plant dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: let the soil dry completely, then water sparingly. Every 2-6 weeks depending on light and season.

When something looks wrong

Soft, mushy leaf bases

Usually: Overwatering — the most common way this plant dies

Stop watering. Remove affected leaves at the base. Repot into dry gritty mix.

Wrinkled, curling leaves

Usually: Genuinely underwatered

Water thoroughly once; leaves plump up over several days.

Worth knowing

In winter it may need water only once a month. When in doubt, wait another week.
