Marble Queen Pothos Care Guide

Epipremnum aureum Marble Queen

EasyToxic to pets

Marble Queen Pothos (Epipremnum aureum Marble Queen) is hard to kill, which is how most of them die: it tolerates neglect well enough that people stop looking at it. The first thing that usually goes wrong is new leaves coming in mostly green — not enough light to support the white sections. It wants bright indirect light. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright indirect. Needs more light than plain green pothos to hold its white variegation.
Water
Let the top half of the pot dry out. Grows slower than golden pothos, so it drinks less.
Humidity
Any. Unfussy.
Soil
Standard potting mix with added perlite.
Temperature
60-85 °F
Feeding
Dilute balanced feed every 6-8 weeks in the growing season.
Toxic to cats and dogs

Insoluble calcium oxalates. Oral irritation, drooling and vomiting if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Marble Queen Pothos dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: let the top half of the pot dry out. Grows slower than golden pothos, so it drinks less.

When something looks wrong

New leaves coming in mostly green

Usually: Not enough light to support the white sections

Move brighter. Prune back to a well-marbled node.

Brown crisping on the white parts first

Usually: Sun scorch - white tissue burns before green does

Pull back from direct sun into bright indirect.

Worth knowing

The white parts carry no chlorophyll, so this grows noticeably slower than golden pothos. That is normal, not a problem.
