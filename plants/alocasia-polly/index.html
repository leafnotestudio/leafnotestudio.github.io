Alocasia Polly Care Guide

Alocasia x amazonica 'Polly'

TrickyToxic to pets

Alocasia Polly (Alocasia x amazonica 'Polly') has a reputation for being difficult and it is earned. It does not tolerate guesswork. The first thing that usually goes wrong is dropping all its leaves in autumn — often natural dormancy, not death. It wants bright indirect light, and genuinely high humidity rather than whatever your living room happens to have. It is toxic to cats and dogs, which is worth knowing before you bring one home.

Light
Bright indirect. No direct sun.
Water
Keep lightly moist in summer; reduce sharply in winter. Hates sitting wet.
Humidity
60%+.
Soil
Airy, fast-draining mix with bark and perlite.
Temperature
65-80 °F
Feeding
Dilute feed monthly in spring and summer only.
Toxic to cats and dogs

High insoluble calcium oxalate content. Significant oral irritation, drooling and swelling if chewed.

Why “water every week” is the wrong advice

How fast a Alocasia Polly dries out depends on its pot size, how much light it gets, the airflow in the room and the time of year. The same plant can want water twice a week in July and once a month in January. A fixed schedule ignores all of that, which is why it is the most common way people drown a houseplant.

What to check instead: keep lightly moist in summer; reduce sharply in winter. Hates sitting wet.

When something looks wrong

Dropping all its leaves in autumn

Usually: Often natural dormancy, not death

Reduce water, keep the corm warm and barely moist, wait for spring.

Fine webbing under leaves

Usually: Spider mites — Alocasia are highly prone

Rinse leaves, treat with insecticidal soap weekly for three weeks, raise humidity.

Worth knowing

Check the undersides of leaves weekly. Spider mites find this plant faster than any other on the list.
